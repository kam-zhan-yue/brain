# Key Concepts

## Stress and Strain

- Stress: The quantity of forces acting on an object that causes deformation and is expressed in Newtons per metres squared
	- Stress is equivalent to the pressure on a solid
	- There are three normal components of stress
	- If a force pulls on an object in an outward direction, it is tensile
	- If a force pulls on an object in an inward direction, it is compressive
	- Forces that act parallel to a surface are called sheer
- Strain: Occurs when a material undergoes stress to the point where it is stretched, squashed, or sheared. It is a unit-less measure of the change in the shape or volume of an object.
	- The relationship between stress and strain is represented by a value called Young's modulus. It is dependent on the physical properties of a material and is determined by a strain gauge.
	- The mineralogy of a rock will exert a strong control on how that rock will deform
	- The relative tendency of a rock to strain in response to stress is called its competence
	- Incompetent rocks are composed of soft, weaker minerals like clay
	- Competent rocks are composed of strong, rigid materials like garnet
## Deformation

- Brittle Deformation: Describes the breaking of rocks as opposed to bending.
- Elastic Deformation: Occurs when the atomic bonds in an object bends. When a force is exerted on an object, its atomic bonds stretch parallel to the maximum tensile force, and contract parallel to the maximum compressive force.
	- If the force is great enough, a fracture will occur
	- Accumulated stress is released, which causes an earthquake
	- Reverts back to its original shape once the force is released
- Plastic Deformation: Occurs when the atomic bonds are broken to allow for rock bending. Materials do not accumulate energy when stressed.
	- Will not revert back to their original shape
	- Occurs in the lower crust or mantle
- Viscous Deformation: Describes the deformation of fluids


# Exam Questions

**What is the difference between the different mechanisms of deformation?**

The main mechanisms of deformation are brittle, elastic, and plastic.
- Brittle deformation describes the breaking of rocks under stress
- Elastic deformation occurs when the atomic bonds in the rock bend, accumulating stress and releasing it upon reverting back to its original shape
- Plastic deformation occurs when the atomic bonds are broken to allow for the bending of rock, without accumulating any energy

**What is the brittle-ductile transition and why does it exist?**

The brittle-ductile transition zone is the zone of the Earth's crust that marks the transition from the upper, more brittle crust to the lower, more ductile crust.

The transition zone exists due to the interaction between pressure and temperature at different depths.
- At shallower depths, rocks are brittle and fracture under stress as pressure and temperatures are lower
- As pressure and temperature increase with depth, rocks become more ductile, deforming by flow rather than fracturing.
