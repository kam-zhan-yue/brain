# Key Concepts

- Plate Tectonics: The Earth is split up into large tectonic plates that are moving
	- Crust: Thinnest outermost layer, is solid
	- Mantle: Parts of it is solid, but the rest of the mantle is somewhat fluid
	- Core: The outer core is liquid, but the inner core is solid because the pressure is high
- Lithosphere: The solid outermost layer, comprised of the crust and the solid part of the upper mantle
- Asthenosphere: The liquid magma layer underneath the lithosphere. It is capable of flowing.

## Earth's Structure

Heavier elements are allowed to sink down, gases bubble up into the atmosphere. The outer and inner core are made up of nickel and iron. In the outer core, temperatures are high that they melt, but the pressure is not enough to form a solid. In the inner core, temperatures are high, but the pressure is high enough to form a solid.

## Plate Movement

![[plate-movement.png]]

- Plates fit together nicely like a jigsaw puzzle
- There are ridges in the world formed by plates moving away from each other
- Magnetic rocks align themselves in one direction. When the rocks harden, the magnetic alignments are fixed.
- Layers of magnetic rock in the ocean floor show shifting directions, showing that Earth's magnetic field actually flips over time
- Fluctuating stripes of magnetic rock can be explained through the plates moving away from each other over time

## Crustal Strength Profiles

A crustal strength profile plots **depth** on the vertical axis and **stress** on the horizontal axis. It shows how strong rocks are at different depths in the lithosphere.

# Exam Questions

**Can you draw a crustal strength profile for oceanic and continental lithosphere and describe why they are different?**

The continental crustal strength profile can be described through the Jelly Sandwich model.
- The lithosphere has a strong, brittle upper crust, a weaker ductile lower crust, and a strong upper mantle
- Like two hard bread layers with a weak, jelly-like layer in the middle

The oceanic crustal strength profile can be described through the Creme Brulee model.
- The lithosphere has a thin, brittle upper crust overlying a soft, weak ductile mantle
- Strength is mostly concentrated in the brittle upper layer. The mantle lithosphere is relatively weak or behaves ductile due to high temperatures or hydration.
