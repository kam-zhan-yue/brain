## Limiting Factors to Growth
- Populations would continue to increase if they had all the resources they require, but there are limiting factors that control population growth.
- Biotic and abiotic factors that prevent the continuous growth of a population and the conditions of the environment that limit the growth of a species

## Carrying Capacity
![[carrying-capacity.png]]
Each environment / ecosystem has a finite capacity for growth. This is known as **carrying capacity** (k), which is the maximum population size of a species that an ecosystem can support indefinitely.

## Natural Resources and Bio-capacity
- Natural resources can be renewable or non-renewable.
	- Abiotic (from the non-living): land, fresh water
	- Biotic (from the biosphere): forests, animals, fossil fuels
- There are two main components in food production:
	- Human Capacity
	- Bio-capacity
		- Generation of an on-going supply of renewable resources
		- Absorption of its spill-over wastes
## Ecological Footprint
- The ecological footprint is a measure of human impact on Earth's environment. It measures:
	- Human demand on nature (quantity of natural resources needed to support people)
	- Bio-capacity (biologically productive area needed for its inhabitants)
- An ecological deficit occurs when our ecological footprint exceeds our bio-capacity
- Earth had an ecological surplus prior to the 1960s

## The Climate System
The Climate is driven by the uneven distribution and movement of solar radiation
- Global circulation from heat transfer in three cells (Hadley, Ferrel, Polar)
- Coriolis force (inertial force) that deflects moving air masses
- Areas of low / high air pressure moving as vortices

### The Water Cycle
Describes the continuous movement of water on, above, and below the Earth
- Evaporation and precipitation through the climate and ocean
- Runoffs and lakes
- Transpiration through plants
- Infiltration and groundwater

### Primary Production and Vegetation
Primary production is the synthesis of organic compounds (creation of organic matter) from atmospheric or aqueous CO2, mostly through photosynthesis
- Productivity (e.g. plant growth) = Light (Sun) + Water + Air / Temperature
- Biome is a regionally distinct biological community that has formed in response to a shared physical climate and has common characteristics for their environment

### Vegetation, Soil, Food
Rocks / sediment react with the hydrosphere and atmosphere in a process called weathering.
- Continued weathering breaks down rocks and provides nutrients from the rocks
- Soil is a mixture of organic matter, minerals, gas, liquids, and organisms
- Soil provides the medium for life (plant growth and new formation of organic matter)

## Ecosystems
An ecosystem is a community of living organisms in conjunction with the abiotic components of their environment interacting as a system or functional unit. Each ecosystem differs in productivity and offers a particular mix of useful resources.
- Ecosystems perform essential functions that allow humans to live on Earth in a variety of ways
- Ecosystems are threatened by the loss of ecosystem services such as:
	- Climate change
	- Soil loss
	- Biodiversity loss

## Impact on Australia
- Australia is extremely vulnerable to disruptions in the food supply through extreme weather events.
	- There is typically less than 30 days supply of non-perishable food and less than 5 days supply of perishable food in the supply chain at any one time
	- Households generally hold only about a 3-5 day supply of food
	- During the 2011 Queensland floods, several towns such as Rockhampton were cut off for up to two weeks
- Australia's international competitiveness in many agricultural markets will be challenged by the warming climate and changing weather patterns
	- Projected to be one of the most adversely affected regions from future changes in climate in terms of reductions in agricultural productions and exports
	- Warmer and wetter conditions elsewhere boost production of key products such as beef and lamb
- If the current rate of climate change is maintained, adaption to food production will be increasingly difficulty and expensive
	- By 2061, Australia's domestic demand for food could be 90% above 2000 levels, with a similar increase in export demand
	- Transitioning to a new, low carbon economy is critical to avoiding the most dangerous impacts of climate change
	- The lower action on climate change is delayed, the more likely it is that progressive, small scale adaptive steps to cope with climate change will become increasingly inadequate
- Current Australian government policy is to increase agricultural production and food exports, specifically in the north
