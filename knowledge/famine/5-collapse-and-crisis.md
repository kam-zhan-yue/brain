## Collapse... Ecocide
- Humans depend on ecosystems and the services they offer. When ecosystems collapse due to overuse, this results in ecocide.
- Ecocide is the extensive damage to, destruction, or loss of ecosystems of a given territory, whether by human agency or by other means.
- One of the main researchers for ecocide is Jared Diamond, who wrote the book 'Collapse'
- Diamond outlines the process of ecocide as
	- Environmental Degradation
	- Population aggregation
	- Increased demand for goods and resources
	- Overshoot of natural resources
	- Loss of resources
## Tikopia Island
- Part of the Solomon Islands, is culturally Polynesian and has 1,700 inhabitants
- Experienced cyclones for several years, including 2015, causing damage to the island and its infrastructure
- Very resilient due to a variety of techniques for fermenting and securing spoiled crops
- Elaborate agriculture, including crop mixing, no burnings, and small-scale farming
- Around 1050, the first people arrived on Tikopia Island.
- This sustainability came at a cost, as celibacy, prevention contraception, abortion, infanticide, sea voyaging, and expulsion with the ideology of zero population growth were embedded in religion to safe guard the island's survival

## Rapa Nui, Easter Island
- Rapa Nui has little forest left, but was once covered entirely by palm forest as evidence of root casts were found throughout the island.
- Rising population growth. resulted in environmental pressure leading to deforestation up until complete deforestation
- At some point, human consumption of the resources on the island would have overcome its carrying capacity, leading to ecocide
- Tribal warfare was also present. Resource depletion ignites conflict.
- The population view of Rapa Nui's history is as follows:
	- 800-900: small colonisation group arrives, population grows slowly
	- 1000: construction of moai begins
	- 1200: growing population begins widespread forest clearance
	- 1400: rate of deforestation peaks, population is as high as 20,000
	- 1680: deforestation nearly complete, triggering clan warfare, famine, and a population collapse
	- 1722: first Europeans arrive and find remnant population of 2,000-3,000

## Maya
- The ancient Mayan civilisation had a socio political organisation with a class society. 70% of the population were farmers.
- There was advancements in writing and science, with complex hieroglyphic systems and a lunar calendar
- Slash and burn techniques were used to clear dense forests, with farmers employing shifting cultivation and mixed garden systems
- Copan is an archaeological site of the Maya civilisation that began settlement in 2,000 BC.
	- No known monuments in Copan dated after 822 AD
	- Some houses found near hillsides buried in debris from erosion, overfarming, and deforestation
	- After 950 - 1000 AD, the number of obsidian blades drop off, indicating population collapse
	- Skulls showing anemia
- Major construction and hieroglyphic texts virtually ceased after 810 AD, possibly due to overuse of resources / deforestation, environmental degradation, or ware
