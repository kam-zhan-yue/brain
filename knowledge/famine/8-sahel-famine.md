## 1968 - 1973 Sahel Famine
- Drought was a major trigger for Sahel's famine. There was decreased rainfall from 1968 and the peak of the drought came in 1973
- Less rainfall resulted in shrinking of rivers and lakes, decreased supply of fish, and expansion of the desert occurred into grazing areas
- Droughts result in:
	1. Severely restricted food production due to reduced supply of water for irrigated crops like rice and reduced output from small farms
	2. Extreme measures to survive as protracted drought saw progressive failure of farmer adaptations over time. People had to eat seeds intended for next year's crops and mass migration occurred to more fertile southern area and towns. Trees were also cut down at an accelerated rate to use as animal feed (increased desertification)
	3. Nomadic pastoralists in Northern areas were the most severely affected, whole hers lost with subsistence and income impacts which resulted in reduced supply of food for nomads, reduced animals for sale, reduced supply of milk for sale and depleted capital stock all causing a collapse of the pastoral economy
	4. Collapse of social structures, there was conflict between pastoralists and farmers, family separations, pastoralists attempted to farm, increased suicide rates, and massive increase of refugees in the area.
	5. Increased mortality, the estimates are uncertain but there were at least 100,000 deaths and huge disease rates, especially in refugee camps where measles were widespread and fatal
	6. Relief operations were too late, and inadequate, despite early warning
- There was a sharp decline in the average food availability per head, but it was also a distributional famine issue.

## Economy of Pre Colonial Sahel
- Sahel crosses many caravan trade routes and trading rites were integral to African empires across 1,000 onwards
- Nomadic pastoralists supplied animals for transport, certain foods, and extracted taxes
- The North: dry desert borders best suited for grazing, received cereals, cotton, dyes, metals, and wood from the South
- The South: wetter savanna areas best suited for settled agriculture, received animals, milk cheese, hides, jewellery, crafts, salt, and dates
- Production systems were flexible to manage climate variability. Herders's response to drought is to be mobile to get water and feed in dry seasons.

## French Colonisation and its Effects
- Direct administration was established in 1900
- The defeat of African empires meant requisition of camels and cessation of trading routes, resulting in reduced trading options for pastoralists and income losses
- Imposition of administrate land boundaries: restricted previous pastures for grazing, restricted range of mobility necessary to adapt to drought
- Profit oriented cash cropping: peanuts were exported to produce vegetable oils in France. Peanuts were cropped with millet to maintain soil nutrition. However, cash crop mean continuous peanut production and subsequent soil nutrient depletion, resulting in successive expansion of farming into marginal areas
- Taxes in cash: Taxes were paid in cash to encourage peanut production.
- Growth of landless agricultural labourers: demise of inter regional trade forced people out of farming and herding. Taxes forced some farmers off the land but they were still required to pay tax and migrated to work on peanut farms
- Changed food balance: production shifting to cash crop with surplus accumulated by state in the form of taxes
- Changed gendered social structure: in pre-colonial times, women controlled agricultural surplus and, thereby, wealth accumulations which was disrupted by taxation. The cash economy intensified competition within large extended families, leading to disintegration into nuclear families, reducing productivity

## Lead up to the Famine
- 1960: Independence from France but still dependent on peanut and cotton exports to Europe and the US and dependent on foreign funds for investment
- 1945-1968: Boom years in peanut production. In late 1960s, the US dominated the world vegetable oil market and France switched from importing peanuts from Sahel to soybeans from the US. In Sahel, the response was to intensify production which resulted in environmental pressure
- Situation prior to 1968 drought: overproduction of cattle and overgrazing, dependence on cash. crop exports to a few buyers with weakening prices, declining terms of trade, increasingly intense and environmentally degrading peanut production

## Causes of the Famine
1. Drought as a trigger which had a severe impact due to land degradation from overgrazing and cash cropping
2. Decline in mutual support mechanisms as kin networks degraded
3. Cash crop farmers couldn't purchase as much food due to terms of trade on world markets
4. Reduced farm production meant decline in wages
