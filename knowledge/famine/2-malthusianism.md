## South Sudan Famine
- In 2017, famine was declared in South Sudan, an oil rich nation with a population of 12 million that has been plagued by civil war since 2013, experienced famine in 1998, an was only declared a nation in 2011
- In 2015, the WFP identified the potential for drought in South Sudan
- A combination of the drought, crop failures, a surge in food prices, and civil war led the UN integrated food security classification to indicate in 2017 that 40% of people living in South Sudan were suffering food shortage

## Malthusianism
- Malthus was an Anglican Cleric that believed that the problem of famine was related to population
- "What most frequently meets our view is our teeming population: our numbers are burdensome to the world. The Earth scarcely can provide for our needs; as our demands grow greater, our complaints against nature's inadequacy can be heard by all"
- Contributed to the idea that 'whereas population had the potential to grow exponentially, the resource base to support that population with food could only grow linearly, thus more slowly'
- When population growth outstripped resources, it was 'balanced' inevitably by 'natural' factors such as disease, famine, and warfare
- The views of Malthus were developed as a response to more socialist and egalitarian arguments post the French Revolution, who claimed poverty and degradation were due to inequalities in wealth rather than a lack of resources
- The Malthusian trap states that resources grow linearly and eventually population growth outpaces resources

### Malthus Checks
Malthus has two checks on population which he believed would stop population growth from increasing so fast that resources cannot keep up.
- Preventative Checks: Reduce birth rates, i.e. postpone marriage
- Positive Checks: Increasing death rates, i.e. war, infanticide, famine

Malthus's idea of preventative checks were always focused on the poor, never the wealthy or religious, centred on chastity and making sure the wealthy retain control.

### Neo-Malthusianism
- For Neo-Mals, the root of the problem is still exponential population growth. They believe that food production cannot keep up with population growth and food shortages are likely to be more frequent and more intense in coming decades
- Solutions for Neo-Mals include lower population growth rates to achieve a balance between population and available food supply

## Economics and Sen's 'Entitlement Approach'
- Argues that a person's 'entitlement to food' comes from four sources
	- Production
	- Trade
	- Labour
	- Gifts
- Famine is not determined by food availability, but a failure to access food at the level of groups and individuals
- The entitlement approach complements economic explanations for famines based on poverty and market failures
- When food production or market supplies of food falls, prices rise and because food is essential for survival, people who can't afford to buy the food they need face starvation

## Famine as an Act of Man
- Famines have always been political and are related to a lack of democracy
- Sen argues that two features of democracy protect against famine; a free press that won't cover up emerging food crises and free, fair elections to ensure state accountability
- A third political factor in contemporary factors is the role of aid donors,  when food aid is not delivered in time to prevent a food crisis, governments may blame international donors
- Food aid has been used as a political weapon such as in 1974, when the US refused to provide food aid to Bangladesh for their continued trade with Cuba in violation with US sanctions

## War and Complex Emergencies
- Famine has been used as a weapon of war since the Middle Ages
- Conditions of famine can be created as unintended consequences of conflict due to the impacts on food production, marketing systems, and relief interventions
	- Farmers are displaced, livestock are killed
	- Trade routes are disrupted, markets are bombed
	- Humanitarian agencies banned from operating in conflict zones

## Malnutrition
- A person is malnourished when 'they don't get enough food or enough of the right kinds of food to sustain good health or work'
- Types of malnutrition include:
	- Dietary deficiencies which result in a lack of micronutrients
	- Secondary malnutrition in which poor health impedes nutrient uptake and causes health issues
- Impacts of malnutrition can be seen in all stages in the life cycle:
	- Babies with a low birthweight have a higher mortality rate
	- Children's growth is stunted, prone to frequent infection, reduced mental capacity
	- Women can develop problems with pregnancy and reduces their ability to care for a child
- Symptoms of malnutrition include:
	- Being underweight
	- Stunted growth
	- Swollen belly (occurs from protein malnutrition)
	- Marasmus (appearing aged, occurs from inadequate calories)
- Malnutrition is responsible for 90% of hunger related deaths where famine is responsible for 10% of hunger related deaths
- The majority of people affected by malnutrition are children and they are the most vulnerable group to famine
