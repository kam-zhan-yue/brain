## Malthus' Abundance Argument Revisited
- Question 1: Is there a clear relationship between population size, food supply, and famine?
	- Data shows that even though nations have food, external factors such as war limits people's access to food
	- Food was grown but the government took control of it, limiting access to food.
	- There is a relationship between population growth, food supply, and famine
	- However, there is no clear evidence that declines in food availability per capita lead to widespread food crisis
	- Conclusions are lacking context
- Question 2: Is food supply subject to the law of diminishing returns?
	- Agricultural yields are increasing, but not as much as used to
	- However, constraints and demands stimulate innovation
	- Yields do diminish, cannot get indefinite increase in food per unit of area farms

## Boserup and the Green Revolution
- Malthus (1798): Tech / innovation of food production cannot keep up with population growth
- Boserup (1965): People will innovate when faced with biophysical constraints
- Agricultural Modernisation Timeline
	- 1800-1900s: Smallholder production on common lands to larger, private holdings
	- 1960s-1980s: Green Revolution, technical innovation (GMOs) and surplus intensification, corporate agriculture for national surplus, and bilateral export
	- 1990s-2000s: Global food production and value chains, production - consumption cycles, processing and ecologies dramatically transforming
- Food Production Solutions up to the 1960s
	- Nutrient decline was overcome with crop rotation, manure, guano, intercropping, etc
	- Techniques such as water pumping, borehole drilling, irrigation channels
- 1960s Onwards
	- Genetic Modified Organisms (GMOs)
	- Borlaug minimised wheat stem rust and other infectious fungi
	- Simple techniques for cross breeding and harvesting and planting seeds produced unusually disease resistant strains of wheat, resulting in wheat yield increasing by 6 fold
- Green Revolution
	- Socio technical invention powered by government investment in research,
	- State enabled market creation, 'modernisation' of the countryside
	- Enrolment of small scale farmers into globalised systems of agriculture
	- Rise of global agribusiness

### Limitations of the Green Revolution
- Impact on poor is less than expected (prosperous commercial mechanised vs. poor subsistence farmers)
- Regional benefits highly uneven
- Political impacts (intensified conflict, increased mechanisation and increased unemployment)
- Drove resource degradation and environmental problems

### Side Effects of Green Revolution
- Fertilisers pollute water and kill beneficial insects
- Irrigation causes salt build ups
- Dominance of monocultures lead to thread of diseases

## Can China Feed Itself?
- China has a long history of famines
	- e.g. 'Great Leap' famine in 1958-1961 (30 million deaths)
	- Population has nonetheless been steadily rising
	- Currently has a low death rate and a low birth rate
- Lester Brown's Neo Malthusian argument is that China's population will rise to 1.6 billion by 2030. Since people are eating more meat, more grain is being used to feed livestock. However, land used for grain production is diverted to other uses. Hence, grain production will fall and China will meet deficits through imports
- Food Production in China
	- Growth in middle class
	- Shifts from plant to animal based foods
	- Increased purchasing power
	- Agricultural GDP grew at an annual rate of 4.6% between 1978 and 2011
	- China has been a net food importer since 2013
- Limits to Food Production
	- China has 20% of the world's population and 35% of its agricultural labour force, but only 11% of the land and less than 6% of its water resources
	- China is a relatively land scarce country
- Managing the Risk of Food Crises
	- Land use reforms: red lines for arable land, restricting loss of farmland near big cities
	- Increasing yields: improved soil management through biotechnology, plant genetics, and breeding resaerch
	- Investments in water resources: water wastage is high and can be reduced, increasing efficiency of water irrigation systems
	- Overseas acquisitions 'land grabs'
- China has enough arable land and water to meet projected demand even at current levels of technology, China's overall food self sufficiency is likely to remain above the 90% level until 2030
