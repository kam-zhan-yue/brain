## Main Topics
- Trade: world trade history, the dependency theory, world trade post colonial period, trade and hunger
- Women and Food Security: status of women in developing countries, women as food producers, women and access to food

## Trade
- Trade is the activity of buying and selling (or exchanging) goods and/or services between people or groups of people
- Pre-Colonisation: trade between regional communities just before and at the advent of European colonisation, e.g. Chinese trade with Southeast Asia in the 1200s
- European Colonisation: enabled the expansion of European economies and provided cheap supply of raw materials and labour
- WST Dependency Theory: perspective on develop that argues that there was no initial state of underdevelopment outside Europe, but that Southern countries become underdeveloped due to colonialist exploitation, both directly and indirectly through trade patterns

### World Trade and the Dependency Theory
- Core countries import raw materials from Southern 'peripheral' countries that are necessary for industrial production in the North
- In terms of food, poor countries export cheap stale / non staple agricultural products in exchange for expensive manufactures and technologies
- Problems with dependency theory: it isn't universally applicable and ignores problems of power and mismanagement within periphery countries like corruption

### Trade and Hunger
- Most poor people buy at least some of their food with income
- International trade is a factor in both incomes and food prices
	- Incomes: opportunities for wages and salaries grow when economies grow
	- Food Prices: increased growth can mean increased investment in agriculture and there fore increased food production
- Hunger is most acute in LDCs, where agriculture is a critical sector
- Some LDCs have increased growth in agricultural exports through investments and commodity price booms

### Price Volatility
- Market connection with increased global food production drives food crises and riots
- Increasing costs of oil and chances of financial crises

## Women and Food Security
- Gender inequalities exist in most societies as they are embedded in patriarchy
- Women supply 43% of the agricultural labour in developing countries
- Women labour demands amplified because of privatisation and ownership along 'male line'
- Women work harder than men due to additional burden of household food preparation, child and aged care, and household chores (limits time spent on food production, education, and work)
- Kenya Case Study: Average woman works 56 hours a week compared to the average man who works 42. Girls do 3.7 times more household work than boys
- Women in agriculture may have less access to productive resources and opportunities than men

### Women and Access to Food
- Women's employment in wage labour improves household's nutritional status. Women spend a higher proportion on food and health care than men
- Rural women in waged work are more likely than men to hold seasonal, part time, low wage jobs
- Women's employment in wage labour and wage rates increase with education

### Women and Nutrition
- Higher nutritional status of women reduces infant mortality and increases child survival and. health
- Men eat more and better than women. In Bangladesh, the risk of death due to malnutrition is twice as high for girls than boys

### Solutions
- Eliminate discrimination in access to farming inputs
- Formal and cultural rights to land
- Target agricultural extension services to women
- Increased secondary and tertiary training for women
- Remove barriers to participating in wage labour
