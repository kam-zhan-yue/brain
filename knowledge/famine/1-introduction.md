## Famine
- Defined as "the absolute inaccessibility of food to an entire population or sub group of a population, potentially causing death in the short term
- The IPC declares a famine when at least 1/5 households face an extreme lack of food, more than 30% of children under 5 are suffering from acute malnutrition, and at least 2 out of 10,000 people are dying every day

## Causes of Famine
There is never a single cause of famine, instead complex and interrelated processes drive famines.

## Features of Famine
- They are an exceptional event
- Increase the death rate
- Result in severe social disruption (loss of livelihoods, starvation, widespread illness, etc)

## Long Term Effects of Famine
- Changing rural production
- Social marginalisation and poverty
- Unequal access and use of food and malnutrition
- Violent and unaccountable political regimes
- Demographic change
- Regional. Market Shifts

## Theories of Famine
- Relative Abundance Argument
	- There are too many people and not enough food
	- Agricultural production cannot keep up with population growth in some places
	- Environmental degradation causes food scarcity
- Distribution Argument
	- Scarcity is the result of different kinds of social processes
	- The power dimensions that influence the equitable distribution of food who it it most or those who hoard it
- Political Ecology Argument
	- Political and environmental change that influences access to, use of and control over food, food production and exchange
	- Attempts to understand how / why famines emerges from power, control, and authority
	- Assumes that certain political and economic conditions drive certain types of power, control and authority thus limiting the ability of the poor and other groups to access food
	- Tries to understand the farmer's perspective, struggles, and resistance
