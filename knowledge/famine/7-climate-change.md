## Climate

### Climate Change in Earth's History
- Climate is highly variable over many timescales
- Changes in the Sun's magnetism produces a greater number of sun spots, causing solar eruptions of particles and radiates more energy
- Sun's energy output only changes by up to 0.15% (smaller effect than recent climate change)
- Volcanic sulphate aerosols in the stratosphere reduce solar radiation to Earth's surface for years, reducing global surface temperatures

### Quaternary Climates
- Quaternary: The last 2.6 million years of Earth's history
- Characterised by ice ages and human history
- As much as 30% of land surface was covered in ice during Quaternary glacial periods
- We know from archives of ice cores, tree rings, corals, sediment cores, etc

### Glacials
- Ice volume + sea level = shelves exposed
- Corridors for biotic exchange of terrestrial organisms during glacial maxima and for marine life during interglacial
- Barriers for terrestrial life during the interglacials

## Climate Change in The Sahara
- In the African Sahel, the weather is largely dry for most of the year, but there is a rainy season of a few months in which most of the annual rainfall occurs.
- Climate records show that 2-3 successive years of abnormally low rainfall (drought) typically alternative with several successive years of average or higher than average rainfall
- Africa's Sahel region is sandwiched between the continent's monsoon forests and the Sahara desert and has a band of wet dry tropical climate with a history of wet and dry periods that are decades long
- Although there has been at least one severe Sahelian drought in each of the last four centuries, the drought of the late twentieth century, which began in 1968 was an environmental disaster of the first magnitude
	- Famine killed 100,000 people and left 75% of the population dependent on food aid
	- Severely impacted agriculture, livestock, and human populations
- Recent studies suggest that drought in the Sahel is linked to large-scale atmospheric circulation changes that are produced by multi-decade variations in global sea surface temperature.
	- A pattern of sea surface temperatures develops int he Pacific, Atlantic, and Indian oceans that weakens the African monsoon circulation
	- This reduces or eliminates the Sahel's brief rainy season
	- Since the pattern can persist for a number of years, the drought produced can be long-lasting
- Presently, rainfall in the Sahel is below historical levels, although drought is not severe

### Issue 1: Famine in the Sahel
- In the 1970s, 50 million people were affected by drought
- Droughts occurred due to the feedbacks between ocean, vegetation, and atmosphere
- The feedback creates two distinct climate regimes: a wet and dry Sahel
- Climate impinges upon communities in desert margins by not only controlling water availability for plants but also by influencing the physio chemical processes of the land upon which plants grow

### Issue 2: Desertification
- Desertification is land degradation in which a relatively dry area becomes increasingly arid and typically loses its bodies of water as well as vegetation and wildlife

### The Future of the Sahel
- Sahel climate is highly variable over many timescales and is very vulnerable
- Vegetation from 1982-2015 shows recent 'greening'
- There are many ways to counter desertification
- To produce better forecasts, we need to understand what drives rainfall variability over long timescales and large regions
