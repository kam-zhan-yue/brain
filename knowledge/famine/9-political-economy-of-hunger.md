## The Political Economy of Hunger
- The political economy theory focuses on: the roles of the state, political interests, and economics in the production, exchange, and distribution of resources, rights and opportunities
- The abundance theory focuses on the relationship between population and food supply
- The distribution theory focuses on the distribution of food, who produces it, who gets it, and why distribution is uneven

### The Distribution Theory
- Famines often take place in situations of moderate to good food availability, without any significant decline of supply per head
- Famines and food insecurity arise from the uneven distribution of food: there is enough food, it's just that not everyone gets it. The uneven distribution is a socially created problem
- The distributional causes of famine and food insecurity are rooted in broader economic, social, cultural, and political factors
- A perfect distributional theory of famine would be able to explain everything, but evidence and causality are too complex to model

### The Political Ecology Theory
- Political, economic, and environmental change influences access to, use of, and control over food, food production, and food exchange
- Global, national, and local politics and economy influence over food access, use, and control

### The Issue of Scale
- Scales of analysis include global, regional, national, provincial, local, household, and individual
- Global political economies: colonial, post colonial food production and exchange,  trade of raw / processed commodities
- National political economies: inherited / new state laws, institutions, state political system and economic structures, educational, technological capacity
- Local and Household political economy: type of livelihood, social, cultural relations / access / use (gender relations), types of resources / environment

### Livelihood and Poverty
- The cyclical nature of poverty (poverty traps), economic growth does not necessarily reduce poverty or food insecurity
- Poverty tends to be chronic (chronically poor people are more or less permanently food insecure)
