## Main Topics
- How much food do we need?
- How much food is there?
- Why are some stuffed and other starved?
- Is increasing food production the answer?

## How much food do people need?
- We all have basic requirements to grow and be health. These requirements vary with age, sex, size of body, and energy use
- The FAO calculates average minimum dietary energy requirement (MDER) for all people globally as 1,850 calories per person per day. This differs per country
	- Australia: 1,950
	- Netherlands: 2,000
	- Occupied Palestine: 1,690
	- Timer-Leste: 1,700
## How much food is there?
- Global average need: 1,850 calories / person / day
- Global average supply (2021): 2,960 calories / person / day
- Hence, we can see that aggregate global supply of food is sufficient
- However, the problem is distribution and lack of access

## Food Regimes
- 1st (Colonial) Food Regime (1800s - 1920s): Food / raw commodity exports from colonies to European / New World industrial expansion (sugar, cacao, slaves)
- 2nd (Post-Colonial) Food Regime (1950s - 1970s): Food flows South to North for 'export-oriented' industrialisation in Third World
- 3rd Corporate Food Regime (1980s - today): Emerging agrifood monopolies and mega-profits of corporations, globalised meat production, and growing links between food and fuel

### Obesity and Food Insecurity
- In 2022, 2.5 billion adults were overweight, with 890 million living with obesity
- In USA, 42% of adults are obese
- 1 of 3 obese adults are food insecure
- Food Deserts: Processed 'fast food' is cheaper, unhealthy, and accessible
> More than 29 million Americans live in "food deserts", meaning they do not have a supermarket within a mile of their home if they live in an urban area, or within 10 miles of their home if they live in a rural area, making it challenging to access healthy, affordable food

## Increasing Food Production
There is still expected to be 653 million malnourished people in the world by 2030. Corporate Food Regimes (CFR) aims to increase production through:
- Expansion of farmland area
- Increased efficiency of production
- Higher yields per unit of land through modern farming systems and plant research
However, as the CFR is often more about money than food, it drives uneven trade, land grabs, poverty, and food insecurity.

### Opinions of Theorists
- NeoMalthusians (Pessimists): Limits to population expansion have been reached or have already been exceeded
- NeoMalthusians (Optimists): Argue there will. be improved efficiencies and better distribution
- Critical Political Ecologists: Argue there are other ways to produce food more sustainably and socially just
