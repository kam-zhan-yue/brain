## Agrarian Transitions (Food Regimes)
- In the last two centuries, global economic change has accelerated an 'agrarian transition': a process whereby national and local agriculture, economise, and environments move from being largely rural to mainly agro-industrial
- Rural outcomes: cash cropping, wage labour, land claims, and loss of land, degradation, agro-ecology, food insecurity
- Global, rural driver: agro-industrial expansion, intensified farming, mono cropping, market expansion
- Impacts of agro-industrial production: remittances and benefits for female migration and agricultural transition in the Phillipines
- Impacts of agro-ecological production: farms under 2 hectares produce 30-34% of global food supply on 24% of gross agricultural area

## Subsistence to Agro Industrialism
- Decline in agro-ecological diversity, rise in endangered species, and threats of extinction and rise in food security among the poor
- Loss of agro biodiversity in intensive cash cropping
- Increase in greenhouse gas emissions
- Facilitates more chance for female migration
- Increase in soil diversity

## New Agro-Ecology
- Revitalising agro-ecology via global Food Sovereignty Movements: 'the right of peoples to healthy and culturally appropriate food produced through ecologically sound and sustainable methods, and their right to define their own food and agriculture systems'
- Nyeleni Declaration on Agro-ecology
	- Its political, grass-roots international movement now encompassing varied small scale producers
	- A socio-ecological science protecting local land, knowledge, seeds, and crops
- Agro-ecology systems often remain the primary form of food production; it is self sufficient, less expensive, semi-autonomous, etc
