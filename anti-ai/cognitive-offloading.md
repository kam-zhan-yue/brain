[AI Tools in Society: Impacts on Cognitive Offloading and the Future of Critical Thinking](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4698/15/1/6)

*Summary*
- Critical thinking is defined as the ability to analyse, evaluate, and synthesise information to make reasoned decisions. It involves various cognitive processes, including problem-solving, decision-making, and reflective thinking.
- Growing evidence shows that over-reliance on these tools can lead to cognitive offloading. Cognitive offloading occurs when individuals delegate cognitive tasks to external aids, reducing their engagement in deep, reflective thinking.
- Critical thinking requires active cognitive engagement to analyse and evaluate information effectively. This is not limited to AI, Sparrow et al demonstrated that the availability of information through search engines can affect memory retention and the inclination to process information deeply.

*AI and Cognitive Offloading*
- Gerlich highlights how increased rust in AI tools can result in greater cognitive offloading, which in turn reduces engagement in critical thinking.
- One of the most notable impacts of AI on cognitive functions is related to memory. Sparrow et al introduced the concept of the 'Google effect', suggesting that the availability of information at our fingertips reduces the need for internal memory retention. This phenomenon, known as 'transitive memory' implies that people are more likely to remember where to find information rather than the information itself.
- Research by Risko and Gilbert highlights that frequent interruptions and multitasking, often facilitated by AI tool, can impair cognitive performance and decrease the quality of attention.
- Cognitive load theory posits that the human cognitive system has limited capacity, and reducing cognitive load can enhance learning and performance. AI tools can reduce cognitive load, such as handling scheduling and reminders and providing feedback and support. However, over reliance on AI tools for routine and complex tasks can lead to a decline in cognitive abilities.
- When individuals rely on AI to make decisions, they make not fully understand the underlying processes and criteria used by the AI to make decisions. This 'black box' problem can reduce critical engagement and accountability, as individuals may blindly trust AI recommendations without questioning or evaluating them.

*Impact of AI on Critical Thinking*
- Individuals who depend too heavily on AI to perform analytical tasks may become less proficient at engaging in deep, independent analysis. This reliance can lead to a superficial understanding of information and reduce the capacity for critical analysis.
- AI tools like recommendation systems can filter out unreliable sources and highlight high-quality content. However, they can inadvertently reinforce biases and limit exposure to diverse perspectives.
- Algorithms used by AI systems can create echo chambers by recommending content that aligns with the users' existing beliefs. This is known as algorithmic bias and can hinder critical evaluation by encouraging confirmation bias.
- Intelligent tutoring systems (ITSs), which simulate one-on-one tutoring experiences through AI algorithms, have been shown to improve learning outcomes, particularly in STEM fields, but they can contribute to cognitive offloading, where students rely on the system to guide their learning rather than engaging actively with the material.

*Materials and Methods*
- To measure critical thinking, the study employed the Halpern Critical Thinking Assessment (HCTA) which assesses verbal reasoning, argument analysis, hypothesis testing, and likelihood estimation. It has been widely used in educational and psychological research to analyse critical thinking skills.
- 666 participants were sampled on their AI tool usage, cognitive offloading (use of digital devices for memory and problem-solving tasks), and critical thinking.
- Analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to compare critical thinking scores across different levels of AI tool usage. The study found that higher AI tool usage is associated with reduced critical thinking abilities. These findings align with the theories of cognitive offloading.
- Education level, age, and occupation were found to have significant effects. Participants with advanced education levels exhibited higher levels of critical thinking.
- However, AI tool use stood out as the most important predictor, meaning it has the greatest impact on the model's ability to predict critical thinking scores. Education and deep thinking activities have moderate importance and their impact is less than AI tool use.
- Participants were interviewed and replied with:

> I sometimes wonder if AI is subtly nudging me towards decisions I wouldn't normally make

> I find myself using AI tools for everything - whether it's finding a restaurant or making a quick decision at work. It saves time, but I do wonder if I'm losing the ability to think things through as thoroughly as I used to.

> While I use AI tools regularly, I always make sure to critically evaluate the information I receive. My education has taught me the importance of not accepting things at face value.

> I don't really think critically when using AI, I just follow what it suggests.

- Across all age groups, there was shared concern about the long-term impact of AI tools on cognitive skills. One participant remarked: "It's great to have all this information at my fingertips, but I sometimes worry that I'm not really learning or retaining anything. I rely so much on AI that I don't think I'd know how to solve certain problems without it."
