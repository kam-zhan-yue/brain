OpenGL expects all the vertices that we want to become visible to be in normalised device coordinates after each vertex shader run. That is, the x, y, and z coordinates of each vertex should be between `-1.0` and `1.0`. Coordinates outside this range will not be visible.

We usually specify the coordinates in a range (or space) we determine ourselves and in the vertex shader transform these coordinates to normalised device coordinates (NDC). These NDC are then given to the rasteriser to transform them to 2D coordinates / pixels on your screen.

Transforming coordinates to NDC is usually accomplished in a step-by-step fashion where we transform an object's vertices to several coordinate systems before finally transforming them to NDC. The advantage of transforming them to several *intermediate* coordinate systems is that some operations / calculations are easier in certain coordinate systems.

There are a total of 5 different coordinate systems:
- Local space
- World space
- View space
- Clip space
- Screen space
