Story points are a relative estimation method in Agile that assesses effort, complexity, and risk for backlog items.
- Story points, instead of hours, helps to encourage team collaboration, reduces emotional bias, and improves forecasting (?)
- Estimation is one of the most difficult parts of software development
- Teams assign story points based on collective experience
	- A simple bug fix might be 1 point
	- A complex feature could be 8 points
- Factors that influence story points include the complexity of the work, the amount of effort required, potential risks, and any dependencies or unknowns. 

Traditional software teams give estimates in a time format.
- Dates don't account for the non-project related work that inevitable creeps into our days, such as emails and meetings
- Dates have an emotional attachment to them (?)
- Once you agree on the relative effort of each story point value, you can assign them quickly without much debate
- Story points reward members for solving problems based on difficulty, not time spent

Teams shouldn't use story points as a measure of productivity, but to understand the size of work and the prioritisation of the work.

### Planning Poker
Teams starting out with story points use planning poker. 
- The team will take an item from the backlog, discuss it briefly, and each member will mentally formulate an estimate
- Then everyone holds up a card with the number that reflects their estimate
- If everyone is in agreement, great! If not, take time to understand the rationale between different estimates
