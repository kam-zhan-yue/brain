## Gravel or Road Bike?
### Tyres
A standard width for a tyre is 28mm. Road bikes have thinner, smoother tyres. Gravel bikes have wider, threaded tyres and are more comfortable on bumpier terrain. Gravel tyres are a minimum of 40mm and are slower than road bikes, but not dramatically. You won't feel the difference until above 25km/h. You can put road tyres on a gravel bike, but not gravel tyres on a road bike.
### Gears
Gravel bikes generally only have 1 chain ring, but road bikes have 2 chain rings. The gears on gravel bikes are tailored for going slower. When the speeds increase, you might find that you run out of gears. The jump between the gears is larger on gravel bikes.
### Geometry
Gravel bikes tend to be more stable than agile. Road bikes are agile and feel more responsive. Gravel bikes puts the rider in a more upright position, which is more comfortable but not aerodynamic.
### Weight
Road bikes tend to be lighter by quite a margin (normally a kilo). Road bikes don't need to endure the same amount of bumps.
### Pedals
Gravel pedals are smaller and double-sided. The shoes are more comfortable. Road pedals are single sided. The cleats that attach to the shoes are huge and not made for walking. 
### Mounting Points
Gravel bikes tend to have more mounting bikes as they are designed for biketouring / bikepacking / commuting. Road bikes are designed to ride on roads and only for roads. 

## All Road Bikes
Road bikes that have greater tyre clearances that are positioned to be between a road bike and a gravel bike.
- Gravel bikes have a slack geometry to take care of rough terrain. All-road bikes have a true road geometry, which will feel like a road bike
- An all-road bike will have only a couple of mounting points
- All-road bikes are not a new idea, but bikes have become more specialised in recent years, so gravel bikes are really gravel bikes

I think what is important is to write down a set of requirements, then figure out what bikes fit it.
